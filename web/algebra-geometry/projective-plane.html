Points at infinity

A plane, a hemisphere, and one line through the centre.

Every line through 0 marks one point on the hemisphere and one point on the plane above it. Tip the line until it is horizontal: the point on the plane runs off to infinity, and the point on the hemisphere reaches the rim.

In space

Gold: the equator z = 0. Translucent: the affine plane z = 1, touching the hemisphere at its top. Dashed: hidden behind the sphere. Drag to rotate the view.

The affine plane z = 1, from above

Why is this the projective plane?

Projecting from the centre of the sphere is called central or gnomonic projection. It sends great circles to straight lines, which is why it suits projective geometry. It is not the stereographic projection of the Riemann-sphere lesson, which projects from a pole and sends circles to circles.

In coordinates: the line through 0 and (x, y, z) is the projective point [x : y : z]. For z ≠ 0 it is the affine point (x/z, y/z); the points with z = 0 form the line at infinity.
