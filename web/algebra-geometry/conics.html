What is the map, and why is the choice of line arbitrary?

Write L as x cos α + y sin α = d. The right panel shows the image of the left one under (x, y) ↦ d (x, y) / (d − x cos α − y sin α). In homogeneous coordinates this is the invertible linear map [x : y : z] ↦ [d x : d y : d z − x cos α − y sin α], a change of coordinates of the projective plane. It sends lines to lines, moves every point along its ray from the centre of the ellipse, and sends exactly the points of L to infinity. When the line is far away the map is nearly the identity.

Nothing distinguishes one line of the projective plane from another: any line can be taken to any other by such a map. Removing a line leaves an affine plane, and which line is removed is a choice. The map also does the opposite job for the old line at infinity: it becomes the ordinary line drawn dashed on the right. A conic is one projective curve; "ellipse", "parabola" and "hyperbola" record how it meets the chosen line: in no real point, in one point counted twice, or in two points.

Over the complex numbers every line meets the conic in two points counted with multiplicity, the first case of Bézout's theorem. An ellipse also has two points at infinity; they are complex conjugates.

The image conic has equation (d x / a)² + (d y / b)² = (d + x cos α + y sin α)², with a = 1.5 and b = 1. Its discriminant is −4 d² (d² − h²) / (a b)², where h² = a² cos² α + b² sin² α: negative, zero or positive as d is greater than, equal to or less than the distance h of the tangent line.
