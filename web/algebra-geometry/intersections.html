Equations and intersections

Where did the intersection go?

A real picture can lose an intersection in two different ways. Complex coordinates and projective coordinates answer different questions.

The real plane

The circle has equation x² + y² = 1. Drag the line or use the slider.

The complex x-coordinate

The same two roots can leave the real axis without disappearing.

What stays constant?

Substituting y = h gives x² = 1 − h². There are two roots over ℂ, counted with multiplicity. At h = ±1, one root counts twice. A real-locus drawing cannot display a nonreal point of ℂ².

These examples illustrate Bézout's qualifications: algebraic closure, projective completion, and multiplicity. Curves with a shared component require a different discussion.
