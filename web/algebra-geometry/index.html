The Erlangen Program
Mathematical exhibits

See the algebra move.

Eight small explorations of intersections, perspective, arithmetic, and functions. Each runs locally without a network connection. Each exhibit includes the mathematical explanation behind its picture.

Slides and handouts for the talks

These exhibits accompany two talks. The slides are what was shown; each handout has the same pictures with the explanations written out.

When Geometry Becomes Algebra

Equations and ideals, the Nullstellensatz, points at infinity and Bézout; the theorems of Pappus, Pascal and Desargues, and how incidence alone builds a number system.

Slides (PDF) · Handout (PDF)

When Functions Become Algebraic Objects

Function fields over ℚ, ℝ and ℂ, branching and Riemann surfaces, why exp and sin satisfy no polynomial equation, and a sketch of the transcendence of e and π.

Interactive exhibits

Where did the intersection go?

Move a line through a circle. Then watch an affine intersection pass through infinity.

A plane, a hemisphere, and infinity

Tip a line through the centre of a hemisphere until its point on the plane runs off to infinity. Parallel lines meet on the rim.

Which line is at infinity?

Drag a line across an ellipse. Sent to infinity, it turns the ellipse into a parabola, then a hyperbola.

The count is the product of the degrees

Move a curve. Intersections merge, turn nonreal, or hide at infinity; the total never changes.

A planar mystery, a spatial proof

Rotate two perspective triangles and find their common axis. Project the configuration back into a plane.

Add and multiply with a ruler

Parallels turn the points of a line into numbers. Pappus's theorem is the law ab = ba.

Follow a square root

One trip around zero exchanges the roots. A second trip restores them.

Infinitely many inputs, one output

The exponential wraps vertical lines around circles: the reason it satisfies no polynomial equation.

Try predicting what will happen before moving a control. Changing a view should reveal an invariant, rather than merely change a picture.
